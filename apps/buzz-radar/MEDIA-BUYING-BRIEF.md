# Media buying for video games: a learning brief

*Written for Buzz Radar users. Researched November 2026. Source pages were found and read through web search results; outbound page fetching was blocked in the research session, so figures are quoted as the cited outlets reported them.*

## 1. What "media buying" means in games

Media buying is the part of marketing where you pay for attention. In games it covers three things:

- **Ads**: YouTube pre-rolls, social ads, app-store ads. Mobile games call this *user acquisition* (UA).
- **Creator sponsorships**: paying YouTubers, TikTokers and streamers to feature the game.
- **Placements**: trailer slots in showcases such as Summer Game Fest, and featured store positions.

Marketers split attention into *paid* (bought), *owned* (your store page, trailer, Discord) and *earned* (reviews, organic streams, memes). Games lean on creators more than most products do, because watching someone play is close to a free demo.

## 2. The creator deal menu

| Deal type | How it is paid | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Integration | Flat fee, usually priced as a CPM (cost per 1,000 expected views) | 30 to 90 second read inside a normal video |
| Dedicated video | Flat fee, often 2 to 3 times an integration | The whole video is about the game |
| Sponsored stream | Per hour, or per average concurrent viewer (CCV) per hour | Twitch, YouTube Live, Kick |
| Affiliate / rev share | % of sales through a code or link | Epic's Support-A-Creator pays 5% of purchases made with a creator's code ([Epic](https://www.epicgames.com/help/c-34406160/a19029301)) |
| Performance marketplace | Per qualifying new player | StreamElements sponsorships pay for new installs, and since 2025 appear inside Twitch's dashboard ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2025/02/25/stream-elements-sponsorship-opportunities-twitch-streamer-dashboards/)) |
| Key distribution | Free keys only | Keymailer, Lurkit and Woovit let creators request keys; coverage is not guaranteed ([Keymailer FAQ](https://account.keymailer.co/faq/1)) |
| Twitch Drops | In-game rewards for viewers | Viewers link accounts and earn items by watching Drops-enabled streams ([Twitch docs](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/drops/technical-guide/)) |

## 3. What it costs

Nobody publishes audited rate cards. Most figures come from agencies and marketplaces, so treat them as ranges:

- **YouTube gaming integrations** are commonly quoted at roughly $10 to $30 CPM, with dedicated videos at about 2 to 3 times an integration ([Influencerfee, an agency guide](https://influencerfee.com/blog/youtube-integration-vs-dedicated-video-pricing/)).
- **Twitch sponsored streams** are often quoted around $1 per average concurrent viewer per hour, with a $0.50 to $2.50 spread ([StreamScheme rate guide](https://www.streamscheme.com/twitch-sponsorship-rate-card/)).
- **Top tier**: in 2019 the Wall Street Journal reported that streamers with 15,000+ concurrent viewers could earn $25,000 to $35,000 an hour on launch days, and the biggest names up to $50,000 ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/top-streamers-said-to-earn-50-000-an-hour-playing-new-1834898693)). Reuters reported EA paid Ninja about $1M to promote Apex Legends' launch. EA did not confirm the figure, but it described "paid engagements with some content creators" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/ninjas-sponsored-apex-legends-launch-stream-cost-ea-dollar1-million-reuters-report-says)).
- **Showcase slots**: Summer Game Fest 2024 main-show trailers were reported at $250,000 for one minute, rising to $550,000 for 2.5 minutes. Some trailers aired free as editorial picks ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/summer-game-fest-sgf-prices-trailer-cost-e3-keighley-1851523472)).
- **Trend**: Digiday reports that big multi-million-dollar creator contracts are drying up, and money is moving to smaller and mid-size creators ([Digiday](https://digiday.com/marketing/as-big-contracts-dry-up-for-top-gaming-creators-smaller-to-mid-sized-influencers-see-opportunity/)).

## 4. The always-on buyer: RAID: Shadow Legends

RAID shows how far mobile publishers will take sponsorship. Analytics firm BuzzGuru estimated that Plarium spent about $8.66M promoting RAID through YouTube creators in six months of 2021, and that most of RAID's YouTube mentions in that period were sponsored ([Game World Observer](https://gameworldobserver.com/2021/12/24/plarium-spent-8-66-million-on-youtube-ads-for-raid-shadow-legends-over-last-six-months)). The reads became a meme of their own ([Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/youtubers-sponsor-raid-shadow-legends/)). Plarium also sponsored the esports team NAVI in 2021 ([PocketGamer.biz](https://www.pocketgamer.biz/news/75582/raid-shadow-legends-dev-plarium-partners-with-esports-firm-navi/)). The business logic is mobile free-to-play economics: if a player's expected lifetime spending exceeds the cost of acquiring them, you keep buying.

## 5. Timing: reveal, build-up, embargo, launch, seasons

A premium game typically follows this sequence:

1. **Reveal**, often at a showcase.
2. **Wishlist build-up**: demos, festivals such as Steam Next Fest, and creator previews ([How To Market A Game](https://howtomarketagame.com/2022/07/11/june-2022-steam-next-fest-how-did-these-games-earn-so-many-wishlists/)).
3. **Embargo lift**: reviews and previews may publish from an agreed moment, so coverage arrives as a wall ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/how-video-game-review-restrictions-work-and-dont-1846199977)).
4. **Launch blitz**: paid streams, ads and creator coverage in the first days.
5. **Live-service calendar**: seasons and patches. Some publishers run per-version creator programs. Genshin Impact's 3.0 Twitch program, for example, required a minimum number of streaming hours and offered a $20,000 prize pool ([GameRiv](https://gameriv.com/genshin-impact-version-3-0-creator-program-on-twitch-registration-details-rules-and-rewards/)).

The key lesson for analysis is that **synchronized posting is not proof of payment**, because embargoes and showcases synchronize creators too.

## 6. Mobile UA vs PC and console wishlists

- **Mobile** buys players install by install. Apple's App Tracking Transparency (2021) made iOS targeting and attribution harder. Moloco reported that iOS cost per install for core games rose 78% after iOS 14.6, while casual games got 38% cheaper ([Moloco](https://www.moloco.com/press-releases/moloco-report-since-ios-14-6-advertising-costs-for-casual-games-dropped-38-and-jumped-78-for-core-games)).
- **PC and console** sell once, so the pre-launch goal is wishlists. GameDiscoverCo reported a recent median of about 0.15 first-week sales per wishlist, with very wide variation ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/the-state-of-steam-wishlist-conversions)).

## 7. Does paying creators work?

The best independent evidence I found is a Kellogg School study of Twitch. Its authors estimated a **median ROI of -95%** for sponsored streams, with about 16% of games seeing high positive returns, mostly lesser-known or critically acclaimed titles. They also found **organic streams about four times as effective** as sponsored ones at bringing in players ([Kellogg Insight](https://insight.kellogg.northwestern.edu/article/video-game-companies-are-spending-big-on-sponsored-streams)).

Paid streams are also a small share of most big launches. In H1 2026, sponsored streams were only 1.86% of Resident Evil Requiem's release-week hours watched. In 2025, FragPunk's share was just over 20% ([Streams Charts H1 2026](https://streamscharts.com/news/top-h1-2026-game-launches-publisher), [Streams Charts 2025](https://streamscharts.com/news/sponsored-streams-gaming)).

## 8. What money cannot buy

- **Among Us** came out in 2018 and broke out in mid-2020, after Korean streamers and then Sodapoppin played it ([Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/among-us-improbable-rise-to-the-top-of-twitch-1844986025)).
- **Palworld** passed 2 million concurrent Steam players within a week of early access ([GameSpot](https://gamespot.com/articles/palworld-just-passed-2-million-concurrent-players-on-steam-only-second-game-to-ever-to-do-so/1100-6520583/)).
- **Helldivers 2**'s lead said the team had prepared for about 150,000 peak players ([GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/games/third-person-shooter/helldivers-2-lead-thought-it-would-bang-but-not-that-hard-we-were-prepared-for-having-150-000-players-peak-ccu)).
- **Balatro** spread through streamers and word of mouth ([GameDiscoverCo](https://newsletter.gamediscover.co/p/how-balatro-hit-1-million-sales-in)).
- **Lethal Company** sold an estimated 10 million copies; the developer has not confirmed the figure ([Game Developer](https://gamedeveloper.com/business/lethal-company-sold-an-estimated-10-million-copies)).

**Controversy** also drives attention. Hogwarts Legacy set a Twitch record for a single-player game despite boycott calls ([GamesRadar](https://gamesradar.com/hogwarts-legacy-topples-cyberpunk-2077-to-smash-twitch-record-despite-controversy)). Valve now detects anomalous review spikes and can exclude off-topic review bombs from a game's Steam score ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/valve-takes-steps-against-steam-review-bombs)).

## 9. Disclosure rules

- **US (FTC)**: creators must disclose any material connection to a brand, including payment, free products and relationships. The disclosure goes with the endorsement and uses plain words such as "ad" or "sponsored". In live streams it should be repeated periodically. Platform tools alone may not be enough ([FTC Disclosures 101](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/disclosures-101-social-media-influencers)).
- **UK (ASA/CAP)**: ads must be obviously identifiable, usually with a prominent "Ad" label up front, and brand and creator share responsibility ([ASA](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/recognising-ads-social-media.html)).
- **Platforms**:
  - YouTube shows "Includes paid promotion" when a creator ticks the box, and may add the label itself ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/154235)).
  - Twitch tells creators to use its branded content tool ([Twitch Creator Camp](https://www.twitch.tv/creatorcamp/en/level1/monetization/sponsorships)). Twitch withdrew a stricter 2023 branded-content policy after streamer backlash ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/06/07/twitch-backtracks-on-changes-to-branded-content-rules-after-streamer-backlash/)).

## 10. Reading a timeline like an analyst

For any burst, ask:

1. **When** did it start? A round hour suggests an embargo or premiere.
2. **Who** posted? First-time creators or regulars?
3. **What** do the titles say? Copy-paste phrasing suggests a brief.
4. **Disclosure**: did the posts say they were paid?
5. **Where did the views go?** Evenly spread views suggest a buy. Views concentrated in a few clips suggest virality.

Buzz Radar's signals map to these questions: disclosure, synchronicity, title similarity, creator diversity, new-creator share, platform spread and engagement skew. Use the score to choose what to investigate, then read the posts. View counts can also be partly bought, since YouTube has counted qualifying ad views in public totals ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2011/06/19/youtube-counts-video-ads-regular-views)).
